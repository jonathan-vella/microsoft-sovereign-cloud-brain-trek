A compliance framework is a set of requirements that an organization must or chooses to meet. Some are laws. Others are industry standards or audit programs. This page explains the common ones, how Microsoft organizes the assurances it holds, and what stays your job when you use a cloud provider.

<LearningObjectives>
- Tell a law, a standard, and an audit attestation apart.
- Describe what GDPR, ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS, HIPAA, and FedRAMP cover.
- Name the four segments Microsoft uses to group its compliance offerings.
- Explain what a cloud provider's certification does and doesn't cover for you.
</LearningObjectives>

## Laws, standards, and attestations

Frameworks come in three broad kinds:

- **Laws and regulations** are legal obligations set by governments. Examples are GDPR in the EU and HIPAA in the US. You must comply if they apply to you.
- **Standards** are published sets of controls that an organization can be certified against by an independent auditor. ISO/IEC 27001 is the best-known security standard.
- **Attestations and authorizations** are audit reports or government approvals about a specific service. A SOC 2 report and a FedRAMP authorization are examples.

## Common frameworks

| Framework | Kind | Applies to | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISO/IEC 27001 | International standard | Any organization | Requirements for an information security management system (ISMS) |
| SOC 2 | Audit attestation | Service providers that store or process customer data | Controls for security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy |
| PCI DSS | Industry standard | Anyone who stores, processes, or transmits payment card data | Protection of cardholder data |
| HIPAA | US law | HIPAA covered entities, such as healthcare providers, and their business associates | Protection of health information |
| FedRAMP | US government program | Cloud services used by US federal agencies | Standard security assessment and authorization for cloud services |
| GDPR | EU regulation | Organizations that process personal data of people in the EU | Personal data protection and privacy rights |

For GDPR, Microsoft acts as a **data processor** for its online services, and you, the customer, are the **data controller** ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/compliance/regulatory/gdpr)). The controller decides why and how personal data is processed, and carries most of the obligations.

### EU regulations you'll meet later

Two newer EU laws come up often in sovereignty work. Level 100 covers them in more detail.

- **NIS2** is the EU cybersecurity directive, Directive (EU) 2022/2555. Microsoft Defender for Cloud and Microsoft Purview Compliance Manager both include an NIS2 assessment ([Defender for Cloud standards](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/defender-for-cloud/concept-regulatory-compliance-standards)).
- **DORA**, the Digital Operational Resilience Act, applies to EU financial entities and their critical ICT providers from January 17, 2025 ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/compliance/dora/dora-what-is-dora)). On November 18, 2025, the European Supervisory Authorities published the list of critical ICT third-party providers under DORA. Microsoft Ireland Operations Limited is on it, so it's subject to direct oversight by those authorities ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/compliance/dora/dora-what-is-dora)).

## How Microsoft organizes its compliance offerings

Microsoft groups the compliance offerings for Azure, Dynamics 365, Microsoft 365, and Power Platform into four segments ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/compliance/offerings/)):

1. **Globally applicable**, such as ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 1, SOC 2, SOC 3, and CSA STAR.
2. **US government**, such as FedRAMP High, Department of Defense Impact Levels, and CJIS.
3. **Industry specific**, such as PCI DSS, HITRUST, and HIPAA.
4. **Region or country specific**, such as Germany C5, Spain ENS High, and Australia IRAP.

The Azure portfolio has more than 100 compliance offerings across these segments ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-government/documentation-government-overview-wwps)). Each offering is based on a certification, attestation, authorization, or assessment by an independent auditor, or on contract terms and guidance documents from Microsoft.

### Different clouds, different offerings

The offerings page lists five Azure cloud environments, and an offering may apply to some and not others ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/compliance/offerings/)):

- **Azure**, the global public cloud, sometimes called Azure commercial.
- **Azure operated by 21Vianet** in China, physically separated and independently operated.
- **Azure Government**, five US regions for US government agencies and their partners. Two of them (US DoD Central and US DoD East) are reserved for the US Department of Defense.
- **Azure Government Secret**, three regions for classified Secret workloads.
- **Azure Government Top Secret**, for workloads classified Top Secret.

These environments are early examples of separate clouds built for national requirements. Level 100 shows how Microsoft Sovereign Cloud extends the idea.

## What a provider's certification covers

A provider's certification covers the provider's part of the shared responsibility model. You inherit the controls the platform implements, and you can use the provider's audit reports as evidence. You still configure and operate your own workloads in a compliant way.

Microsoft states it directly: you're wholly responsible for your own compliance with the laws and regulations that apply to you ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/compliance/offerings/)).

<Misconception>
  <Fragment slot="myth">
    Azure is ISO/IEC 27001 certified, so my application is too.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="reality">
    The certification covers the Azure services in its audit scope. Your application, its configuration, and your processes need their own controls, and your own audit if you want certification. Also check that every service you use is in the audit scope.
  </Fragment>
</Misconception>

## Tools that help you prove compliance

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| Service Trust Portal | Download Microsoft's audit reports, certificates, and assessment documents, for example ISO certificates and SOC reports. Requires sign-in. |
| Microsoft Defender for Cloud regulatory compliance dashboard | Continuously assesses your Azure, hybrid, and multicloud resources against standards you choose, such as ISO/IEC 27001, PCI DSS, GDPR, NIS2, and DORA, and shows where you fall short. |
| Microsoft Purview Compliance Manager | Tracks your compliance posture across Microsoft 365 and other services with assessment templates for regulations, and suggests improvement actions. |
