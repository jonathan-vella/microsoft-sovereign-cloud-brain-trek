Level 50 covers the basics that the rest of the course assumes: what the cloud is, how security and compliance work in it, and how Microsoft Azure is organized. It's vendor-neutral where it can be and uses Azure for the examples. Sovereignty itself starts in Level 100. Level 50 only introduces the terms you'll need there, such as data residency, data sovereignty, and sovereign regions.

## Who should take it

- People new to cloud computing, in any role.
- Sales and pre-sales professionals who want the vocabulary to follow customer conversations about sovereignty.
- Technical people from on-premises backgrounds who want a quick Azure refresher before Azure Local and Azure Arc.

If you already hold the Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900) or Security, Compliance, and Identity Fundamentals (SC-900) certification, take the comprehensive knowledge check first. If you score 16 out of 20 or better, go straight to Level 100.

## What you'll be able to do

- Explain IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS, and who is responsible for what in each.
- Compare public, private, hybrid, and multicloud deployments.
- Explain the CIA triad, defense in depth, and the Zero Trust principles and seven pillars.
- Tell data residency from data sovereignty, and describe how Microsoft groups its compliance offerings.
- Describe Azure regions, availability zones, the resource hierarchy, and the main management tools.

Plan for about three to four hours, including the knowledge checks.

## Where this leads

Level 100 builds on three ideas from this level:

- Shared responsibility becomes a question of who operates the cloud and who can access your data.
- Sovereign regions such as Azure Government lead to Microsoft Sovereign Cloud and its three deployment models: Sovereign Public Cloud, Sovereign Private Cloud, and National Partner Clouds ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-sovereign-clouds/microsoft-sovereign-cloud)).
- Hybrid cloud and Azure Arc lead to Azure Local, which runs Azure services in your own datacenter.
