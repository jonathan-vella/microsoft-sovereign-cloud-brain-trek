## Microsoft's five European digital commitments

Microsoft announced [five European digital commitments on April 30, 2025](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-sovereign-clouds/european-digital-commitments), at an Atlantic Council event in Brussels. The commitments are:

1. 40% European datacenter capacity growth over two years, 16 countries and regions, and more than 200 datacenters by 2027.
2. Digital Resilience Promise to contest any order to suspend or stop European cloud operations, with European board of directors oversight under European law.
3. Continue protecting the privacy of European data, building on the EU Data Boundary.
4. Help protect and defend Europe's cybersecurity through an expanded Europe-focused security posture.
5. Strengthen Europe's economic competitiveness, including open source.

The commitments do not replace a customer's own legal analysis. They give architects and policy teams a current Microsoft source for continuity, data protection, cybersecurity, and ecosystem claims in European sovereignty conversations.

## EU Data Boundary

The EU Data Boundary is a geographically defined boundary where Microsoft commits to store and process Customer Data and personal data for covered Microsoft enterprise online services. Professional Services Data is stored at rest for those services. Microsoft states that the commitments are subject to limited circumstances where those data types continue to transfer outside the EU Data Boundary.

![Diagram of the EU Data Boundary showing EU and EFTA countries, covered services, covered data types, and product-specific scope rules.](/images/level-100/eu-data-boundary-2026.svg)

### Geography

The EU Data Boundary consists of the [27 European Union member states plus the four European Free Trade Association countries](https://learn.microsoft.com/privacy/eudb/eu-data-boundary-learn#eu-data-boundary-countries-and-datacenter-locations).

| Group | Countries |
| --- | --- |
| European Union | Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden |
| EFTA | Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland |

Microsoft says the boundary uses or may use Microsoft datacenters announced or operating in Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Spain, Sweden, and Switzerland.

### Services and data types

The EU Data Boundary covers Azure, Dynamics 365, Power Platform, and Microsoft 365 enterprise online services listed in the Microsoft Product Terms as EU Data Boundary Services. The page also covers Professional Services Data at rest for those services.

The documentation distinguishes these data types:

- Customer Data, which is data provided to Microsoft by or on behalf of the customer through use of an online service.
- Personal data in system-generated logs, which Microsoft requires to be pseudonymized.
- Professional Services Data, which is data provided to or processed by Microsoft during consulting and technical support engagements.

System-generated logs are **pseudonymized, not anonymized**. Microsoft explains that anonymization would permanently alter the record and reduce its ability to ensure service quality, reliability, and security.

### Product-specific scope rules

The EU Data Boundary is not enabled the same way for every product.

| Product | Current scope rule |
| --- | --- |
| Azure | Regional services that a customer deploys in an EU Data Boundary region are in scope. Azure non-regional services need service-specific configuration to be in scope. To store Azure Professional Services Data in the boundary, customers must configure Azure Resource Manager to the EU Data Boundary. |
| Dynamics 365 and Power Platform | The tenant and all environments must be provisioned in the "European Union (EU) and European Free Trade Association (EFTA)" Macro Region Geography, and the customer must maintain a billing address in an EU Data Boundary country. Meeting only one condition does not place the environments in scope. |
| Microsoft 365 | Customers with a tenant sign-up location in an EU or EFTA country or region are in scope. Customers who purchased Multi-Geo Capabilities are not in scope, even if the tenant is listed in an EU or EFTA country or region. |

These differences matter during discovery. Asking "Is the tenant in Europe?" is not enough for Dynamics 365, Power Platform, or Microsoft 365 Multi-Geo customers. Asking "Is the workload in an EU region?" is not enough for Azure non-regional services.
